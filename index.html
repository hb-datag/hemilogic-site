The platform

Four functions on one engine

Most neurotechnology does one job. HemiLogic is architected so a single inference engine carries a device across the full arc of care, from the first ambiguous sign to the intervention that follows.

01

Detect

Surface the neurological signal that a periodic clinic visit misses, in the environment where the patient actually lives.

02

Monitor

Track severity continuously as a graded state rather than a binary flag, so change is visible weeks before it becomes an event.

03

Treat

Close the loop. Deliver and titrate intervention against the patient's own measured response, not a population average.

04

Predict

Forecast trajectory and risk with a stated confidence and a stated reason, giving clinicians a window to act inside.

The problem

Neurological care runs on snapshots

A patient with Parkinson's disease may be assessed for twenty minutes twice a year. A seizure is described from memory, days later. Cognitive decline is caught once it is already visible. The disease is continuous; the data is not.

Wearables promised to close that gap and largely have not, for two reasons. The signals are noisy and the states they represent are genuinely fuzzy, so systems tuned to binary thresholds either cry wolf or stay silent. And the models that do perform well are opaque, which means no clinician can defend acting on them.

Our position: a neurological wearable earns clinical use only when it is right about ambiguous states and can explain itself in language a neurologist would accept in a chart note. Accuracy without an audit trail is not a product. It is a demo.

The approach

Fuzzy logic, because neurology is not binary

Symptom severity lives on a gradient. Fuzzy inference is built for exactly that, and unlike a deep network, every conclusion it reaches decomposes into rules a person can read.

Graded, not thresholded

Membership over cutoffs

A tremor is not "present" or "absent." It has a degree. Membership functions let the system represent partial states honestly instead of forcing a false edge.

Transparent by construction

Rules you can audit

Every output traces back to a human-readable rule and the inputs that fired it. Explainability is not a saliency map bolted on afterward. It is the model.

Clinically shaped

Expert knowledge, encoded

Clinician reasoning goes into the rule base directly, so the system starts from established neurology and learns from data rather than replacing one with the other.

Where we are aimed

Indications under evaluation

The platform is indication-led. Form factor, sensing and rule base are selected per condition rather than forced onto one piece of hardware.

Parkinson's disease

Motor fluctuation, tremor and gait, and response to medication across the day.

Epilepsy

Event detection and the harder problem underneath it, forecasting risk before onset.

Cognitive decline

Early signal and progression tracking in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias.

Stroke recovery

Objective measurement of neurorehabilitation outside the therapy session.
